The catalogue — all 50 formulas, and why the tiers exist

A 64-bit float carries only ~16 reliable decimal digits, so every engine here computes in Python's decimal arithmetic with 10 hidden guard digits, and results travel to this page as strings — the moment JavaScript turned them into Numbers, digits beyond 16 would become noise.

What separates the tiers is the order of each formula's error. Instant engines gain digits multiplicatively — Chudnovsky earns ~14 per term, Gauss–Legendre doubles its count per iteration — so 32 decimals costs a handful of steps. Medium engines converge like 1/N³: Nilakantha reached 16 decimals here in 7.9 million terms, but 32 would take ~10¹² — about two weeks of looping. Slow engines converge like 1/N, paying ten times more work for every digit: measured live, Leibniz gives 2 digits at 1,000 terms, 3 at 10,000, 4 at 100,000, 5 at 1,000,000. Statistical simulators obey the 1/√N law — 8 correct decimals would demand ~10¹⁷ samples, so half a million buys one or two digits. And reference entries are the identities, proofs, fixed fractions, duplicates and industrial pipelines that express π without estimating it.
